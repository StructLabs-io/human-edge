# What the research says: the human edge when AI does almost everything

This is the tour: the short version first, then one section per theme. Each section points to a deeper file in `research/` with every study, its numbers and a link. The full source list is in `sources.md`.

Compiled October 2026. The kit cites about 200 sources, most of them published 2025–2026. A separate audit checked about 40 of the key ones against the original papers. None was fabricated; where the audit corrected a number or a grade, this kit uses the corrected version.

**Evidence labels used throughout:**
- **Strong:** several randomized trials or meta-analyses agree, or one large preregistered multi-study paper in a top journal.
- **Moderate:** some trials or robust lab effects, with caveats.
- **Preliminary:** one study, a preprint, or survey data.
- **Weak:** correlational data, very small effects, or practitioner opinion only.
- **Contested:** credible evidence on both sides.

**Where the data comes from.** Mostly the US, UK, EU and China. People in emerging economies tend to trust AI more: 57% versus 39% in advanced economies across 47 countries ([KPMG and University of Melbourne, 2025](https://kpmg.com/za/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025/05/global-study-reveals-trust-of-ai-remains-a-critical-challenge-reflecting-tension-between-benefits-and-risks.html); Malaysia wasn't surveyed). Malaysian survey data shows high comfort with AI too, on a different measure: 37% of Malaysians say they trust AI tools enough not to check their work, against 31% globally (40% across Asia, 23% in the US) ([Ipsos AI Monitor, 2026](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/news/documents/2026-06/AI%20Monitor%202026%20%28English%29.pdf)). So the "people prefer people" premium may be smaller here. `research/business-and-coaching.md` has the Malaysia and regional detail.

## 1. The short version

- **The edges that hold are about who stands behind the work, not about tasks AI can't do.** AI already writes more compassionate-sounding replies and more persuasive arguments than most people. What it can't be is a person who chose to show up, who answers for the outcome, and who has lived the thing.
- **The same words count for less when people think a machine wrote them**, at least in emotional support and care. In one study, 45% of people chose to wait for a human reply rather than get an instant one from AI, and 43% still chose to wait when the wait offered was two years. Those two figures are counted from the authors' public data; the paper reports that the length of the wait, from 2 hours to 2 years, made no difference ([Rubin et al., 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02247-w)). *Strong for that setting.*
- **Letting AI do the core work while you're learning costs you later.** In several studies, people who leaned on AI scored roughly a fifth lower once it was taken away; using AI as a tutor mostly avoided this. *Moderate to strong, scoped to learning.*
- **Wrong AI advice drags people down, experts included.** Inexperienced radiologists were about 80% correct when the AI's suggestion was right, and about 20% when it was wrong ([Dratsch et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.222176)). *Strong.*
- **AI tends to agree with you.** Models side with users about 49% more often than people do, and users like them more for it ([Cheng et al., *Science*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec8352)). *Strong.*
- **Access to AI makes people less willing to say "I don't know".** They answer more, are right less often and feel more confident ([Marcoccia et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13562)). *Preliminary: one preprint, deliberately wrong AI.*
- **Thinking first helps, but it isn't proven to build lasting skill.** *Moderate.*
- **Customers say they want a path to a person.** 64% would prefer companies not use AI in customer service, and 53% would consider switching to a competitor if they found out a company was going to use AI for service ([Gartner, 2024](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-07-09-gartner-survey-finds-64-percent-of-customers-would-prefer-that-companies-didnt-use-ai-for-customer-service)). *Preliminary: stated preference, vendor survey, fielded December 2023.*

## 2. Five edges that hold up

Researchers and practitioners name dozens of "human skills". Merging the overlaps leaves about five clusters, close to MIT Sloan's EPOCH framework (five) and Ethan Mollick's list (four).

| Edge | What it is | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Relational presence** | A person who chose to show up and spend scarce attention on you, in person or over time | Strong for emotional support and care; moderate elsewhere; shrinks as people have good experiences with AI |
| **2. Accountability** | Someone puts their name on the call and answers for it | Strong as a legal or structural requirement in some places; moderate evidence that people value it |
| **3. Lived and field-tested experience** | Pattern knowledge from years in a domain, first-hand information in no dataset, your own voice | Moderate; AI also spreads expertise to novices |
| **4. Judgment, including knowing what you don't know** | Deciding what fits *this* person and situation; knowing when to trust AI and when to check it | Strong that people over-trust wrong AI; contested that judgment is human-only |
| **5. Honest challenge** | Telling someone what they need to hear, including that they're wrong | Strong that AI flatters by default |

**Weaker or contested, but often named:** meaning and purpose, choosing the right problem, initiative and hope, and taste.
**Worth adding for small and medium businesses:** first-hand private information, relationship capital and networks, physical craft, and local and cultural fluency.
→ Every study behind each edge: `research/edges.md`

## 3. What AI already matches (don't claim these as human-only)

AI matches or beats most people on empathic wording, persuasion, moral advice as people perceive it, average creativity, forecasting (likely), and passing as human in a short text chat.
**Where AI still falls short:**
- turning knowledge into outcomes with real people
- running things on its own over long periods
- being reliable when the stakes are high

Human–AI teams on average did *worse* than the better of the two alone, with the losses in decision tasks. Creative tasks did better than decision tasks, but their gain wasn't statistically significant ([Vaccaro et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-02024-1)).
→ The full table, the "label effect", and where AI still fails: `research/ai-already-matches.md`

## 4. What happens when people hand over the thinking

- **Learning:** people who let AI do the core work scored roughly a fifth lower once it was removed. Tutor-style use mostly protected learning, and AI access by itself didn't always hurt. What mattered was how people used it.
- **Judgment:** wrong AI pulls people below their unaided level, and training alone doesn't prevent it.
- **Confidence:** people misjudge how much AI helps them and how well they did.
- **Meaning:** passive AI use at work was followed by lower felt meaning and self-efficacy, and those dips lingered. Using AI to workshop your own ideas looked like working unaided.
- **Ideas and voice:** AI help makes people's work more similar to each other, and in one study pulled Indian writers toward Western style.
- **Not supported:** that AI "rots your brain" or causes damage.

→ Every study, with numbers and counter-evidence: `research/handing-over-thinking.md`

## 5. What helps

The best-supported moves:
- **Write your own answer before seeing AI's.** Strong as a learning mechanism; moderate direct evidence with AI.
- **Get AI's answer independently.** In a fresh chat, without showing it your view.
- **Use AI as a tutor or challenger, not an author.**
- **Record how sure you are, and score yourself against what happened.**
- **Name the exact point where you disagree with AI, and check it.**
- **Habit supports:** self-monitoring shared with someone, if-then plans, and rewarding the comeback after a miss.

Gratitude journals, expressive writing and values affirmation have weak or contested evidence for performance.
→ The practices table, the evidence for each step of the think-first loop, and what keeps a habit alive: `research/what-helps.md`

## 6. Business and coaching

Clients *say* they still want five things from people:
1. someone accountable for the outcome
2. judgment about what fits them
3. trust from someone they know
4. field-tested expertise
5. leadership and development of people

That is stated preference, not proof they'll pay more: some economists call interpersonal skills underpriced.
→ `research/business-and-coaching.md`, including what cuts against the human premium and the Malaysia and Southeast Asia context.

## 7. Body, people and meaning

Judgment runs on a body and a social life. Sleep loss lowers self-control and raises risk-taking. Loneliness is common. People who chose heavier chatbot use reported more loneliness. This kit uses these findings only to shape a few reflection questions. It is not health advice.
→ `research/body-people-meaning.md`

## 9. Myths to keep out of your talks and marketing

- "95% success with an accountability appointment": no primary source.
- The Yale or Harvard written-goals study: no trace it ever happened.
- "21 days to form a habit": contradicted.
- "ChatGPT rots your brain": the authors object to this reading of their study.
- "Empathy and creativity are uniquely human": not as written outputs.
