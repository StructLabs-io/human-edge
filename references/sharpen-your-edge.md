# Sharpen your edge: a think-first practice

For the person who uses AI daily and wants to keep their own judgment sharp. The routine takes 2 to 3 minutes on a real decision, plus 10 minutes a week. Every step is drawn from research on that step, and each step below says which. The routine as a whole hasn't been tested, so present it as a sensible design, not a proven programme. The full evidence is in `research/what-helps.md`.

## How to run this with the person

1. **Ask what they use AI for most** and which decisions matter in their work: client calls, pricing, hiring, proposals, coaching sessions. The practice only matters on real decisions, not on looking up facts.
2. **Do one live round with them now** on a real decision they're facing (the loop below). Doing it once beats explaining it.
3. **Then set it up** (section "Set-up") so it survives week two.
4. **Offer the extras.** The question bank, the extras for big decisions and learning, and the weekly review are things they can come back to.

Keep it light. If they only want the questions, give them the questions. Start with the one-line version and a single if-then cue; add the rest only if they ask. Someone who says "I'll forget by Thursday" needs one rule, not a card.

## The loop

**1. Human first: write your read.**
- Before opening AI, write one line: what you think, why, and how sure you are (0 to 100%). "I don't know yet" is a valid answer and worth writing.
- Two minutes is a commitment device. It makes you hold a view, so AI's answer can't quietly replace it.
- For a bigger decision, give it longer.
- *Why:*
  - Committing first cut copying of misleading AI advice from 47% to 26% ([Yan, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1936522/full)), though a two-week follow-up showed no lasting difference.
  - Access to AI made people far less willing to say "I don't know" (one preprint, where the AI's advice was deliberately wrong; [Marcoccia et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13562)).
  - With 30 minutes of thinking time, seeing AI late beat seeing it early; with 10 minutes, early won ([Zhi, Kumar & Lee, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791796)).

**2. AI in the middle: ask in a fresh chat, without your read.**
- Ask AI the same question cold. Don't paste your answer in first.
- AI tends to agree with whoever it's talking to and anchors on what it's shown, so showing it your read gets you a flattering echo instead of a second opinion. Two independent views are worth more than one view and its echo.
- A temporary or memory-off chat works best, so the AI can't pick up your view from earlier conversations.
- *Why:*
  - Models side with users about 49% more often than people do ([Cheng et al., *Science*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec8352)).
  - Averaging only helps when the two estimates are independent ([Soll & Larrick, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015145)).

*When you (the coaching AI) are also the second opinion:* have the person write their read somewhere you can't see it (a notes app, paper). Give your own view first, with your confidence. Only then ask them to reveal theirs.

**3. Compare.**
- Where exactly do you differ?
- What would settle it: a number to check, a person to ask, a test to run?
- What does AI see that you missed?
- What do you know that it can't: this client, this room, your history with this?

**4. Optional: make AI argue against you.**
- Now paste your read and ask: "Do not agree with me. Give me the strongest case that I'm wrong." This is the one time to show it your view.
- *Why:* an AI arguing the other side reduced over-reliance ([Chiang et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3640543.3645199); weak to moderate evidence).

**5. Human last: decide and own it.**
- Change what the evidence says to change, keep what you have good reason to keep, and put your name on it.
- Watch for the opposite trap too: people who decide first sometimes reject AI advice that was right. Disagree on a specific point you can check, never just because it's AI.
- *Why:*
  - Wrong AI drags even experts down ([Dratsch et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.222176)).
  - Deciding first also makes people discount *correct* advice ([Fogliato et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3531146.3533193)).
  - Committing first didn't stop medical students being swayed by bad AI ([Hayden et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09979-x)).

**6. Note who needs to hear it from you.**
- If the decision affects a person, decide whether they should hear it from you directly: in person, on a call, not in a message AI drafted.
- *Why:*
  - The same words count for less when people think AI wrote them ([Rubin et al., 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02247-w)).
  - Being found out using AI costs more trust than saying so ([Schilke & Reimann, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2025.104405)).

**One-line version for busy days:** "My read is ___, I'm __% sure." Then carry on.

## Question bank

### Daily core (pick up to four, before the first AI use on a real decision)
1. What's my read, in one line, and how sure am I (0–100%)? "I don't know yet" counts.
2. What do I know here that AI can't: about this person, this room, my own history with this? *And what might AI see that I can't?* (The second half matters: people judge their own performance badly when AI is involved; [Fernandes et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2025.108779).)
3. After AI: where exactly do we disagree, and what would settle it? What do I change, and what do I keep?
4. What am I putting my name on, and who needs to hear it from me in person?

### Rotating (one a day, 30 seconds)
5. **Energy:** how did I sleep, and how's my energy, 1 to 5? If it's low, which decision gets a second look tomorrow? (A rule of thumb. Badly sleep-deprived people take more risks; there's no magic number of hours.)
6. **People:** who did I have a real conversation with today? Did I take something to AI that I'd normally take to a person? Who will tell me when I'm wrong?
7. **Meaning:** what did I do today that mattered, and which part was mine before AI touched it?

Background for these three: `research/body-people-meaning.md`.

### For coaches before a client session
8. What's my read on this client right now, before I look at any notes or AI summary?
9. What am I avoiding saying to them? What would I say if I knew they'd thank me later?
10. What will I only notice if I'm fully present in the session?

### For big decisions (pick one or two)
11. **Base rate:** what usually happens in cases like this? (Forecasting training starts here, though its effects are contested; [Chang et al., 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/judgdm/v11y2016i5p509-526_8.html); [Hauenstein et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976241266481).)
12. **What would change my mind?** Name the evidence that would make me drop this read. (Guided reflection; [Lambe et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjqs-2015-004417).)
13. **Premortem:** it's a year from now and this failed. Why? (It surfaces more reasons; there's no evidence it improves accuracy; [Mitchell et al., 1989](https://doi.org/10.1002/bdm.3960020103).)
14. **For a number** (a price, a forecast, a budget): average my estimate with AI's independent one, unless I can say exactly why AI is wrong. ([Soll & Larrick, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015145).)

### When learning something new
15. **Try it before asking AI to explain.** Struggling first, then getting the explanation, beat explanation first ([Sinha & Kapur, 2021](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211019105)).
16. **Ask AI to tutor, not to do.** Try: "Ask me questions until I can do this myself. Don't give me the answer." Tutor-style use protected learning; letting AI do the work cost about a fifth on later tests ([Bastani et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122); [Shen & Tamkin, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20245)).
17. **Treat the strain as the point.** Feeling stretched while learning is often a sign it's working ([Cal Newport, 2025](https://calnewport.com/does-ai-make-us-lazy/)).

### Weekly review (10 minutes, ideally Monday morning)
18. Look back at last week's reads. Only score the ones where the outcome is now known; give each read a "check-by" date when you write it.
19. Score the reasoning separately from the result. A good call can turn out badly through luck, and a bad call can get lucky.
20. Where was I over-sure? Where did AI beat me? Where did I beat it? What one pattern do I see? (Scored feedback on your own track record reduces overconfidence; [Moore et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2525).)
21. Send a three-line summary to one person: a peer, a coach, a partner.

## Set-up (so it lasts past week two)

Most people quit habit apps within the first one to two weeks ([Baumel et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.2196/14567); [Medito study, 2026](https://doi.org/10.2196/79366)). Plan for that up front:

- **One if-then cue,** tied to a moment that already happens: "If I open AI for a real decision, then I write my read first." Have them say it back in their own words. ([Sheeran et al., 2024](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/69905); [Judah et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-8287.2012.02086.x).)
- **A miss plan:** "If I skip a day, the next day I do the one-line version." Missing a day doesn't break a habit; quitting after missing one does. ([Lally et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674).)
- **Count total entries, not streaks.** Never tell someone their streak is broken. Celebrate coming back after a gap: in a study of 54 gym programmes, rewarding people for returning after a miss worked best. ([Milkman et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8822539/); [Silverman & Barasch, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029).)
- **One accountability person,** who gets the weekly three-line summary. Self-monitoring works better when it's shared. ([Harkin et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000025).)
- **Fresh starts:** if they've drifted, restart on a Monday or the 1st of the month. ([Dai, Milkman & Riis, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901).)
- **Where to keep it:** wherever they already write: a notes app, a paper notebook, a spreadsheet, a pinned chat. The best log is the one they'll open.
- **Measure it simply:** entries per week, the share of real decisions with a read written first, and after about eight weeks, how often their confident reads turned out right.

The two-minute default is practitioner advice, not a research finding. Keep it small so it survives busy days.

## Privacy and wellbeing

- Don't put confidential client or customer details into AI tools or shared logs. Write "Client A" instead of names.
- This is a thinking habit, not health or mental-health care. If a question brings up something heavy, talk to someone you trust or a professional.
