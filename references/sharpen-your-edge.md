# Sharpen your edge: a think-first practice

For the person who uses AI daily and wants to keep their own judgment sharp. The routine takes 2 to 3 minutes on a real decision, plus 10 minutes a week. Every step is drawn from research on that step. The routine as a whole hasn't been tested, so present it as a sensible design, not a proven programme.

## How to run this with the person

1. **Ask what they use AI for most** and which decisions matter in their work (client calls, pricing, hiring, proposals, coaching sessions). The practice only matters on real decisions, not on looking up facts.
2. **Do one live round with them now** on a real decision they're facing (the loop below). Doing it once beats explaining it.
3. **Then set it up** (section "Set-up") so it survives week two.
4. Offer the question bank and the weekly review as things they can come back to.

Keep it light. If they only want the questions, give them the questions. Start with the one-line version and a single if-then cue; add the rest only if they ask. Someone who says "I'll forget by Thursday" needs one rule, not a card.

## The loop

**1. Human first: write your read.** Before opening AI, write one line: what you think, why, and how sure you are (0 to 100%). "I don't know yet" is a valid answer and worth writing. Two minutes is a commitment device: it makes you hold a view, so AI's answer can't quietly replace it. For a bigger decision, give it longer. In one study, thinking time of 30 minutes before seeing AI paid off, while 10 minutes didn't.

**2. AI in the middle: ask in a fresh chat, without your read.** Ask AI the same question cold. Don't paste your answer in first. AI tends to agree with whoever it's talking to and anchors on what it's shown, so showing it your read gets you a flattering echo instead of a second opinion. Two independent views are worth more than one view and its echo. A temporary or memory-off chat works best, so the AI can't pick up your view from earlier conversations.

*When you (the coaching AI) are also the second opinion:* have the person write their read somewhere you can't see it (a notes app, paper), give your own view first with your confidence, and only then ask them to reveal theirs.

**3. Compare.** Where exactly do you differ? What would settle it: a number to check, a person to ask, a test to run? What does AI see that you missed? What do you know that it can't (this client, this room, your history with this)?

**4. Optional: make AI argue against you.** Now paste your read and ask: "Do not agree with me. Give me the strongest case that I'm wrong." This is the one time to show it your view.

**5. Human last: decide and own it.** Change what the evidence says to change, keep what you have good reason to keep, and put your name on it. Watch for the opposite trap too: people who decide first sometimes reject AI advice that was right. Disagree on a specific point you can check, never just because it's AI.

**6. Note who needs to hear it from you.** If the decision affects a person, decide whether they should hear it from you directly: in person, on a call, not in a message AI drafted.

**One-line version for busy days:** "My read is ___, I'm __% sure." Then carry on.

## Question bank

### Daily core (pick up to four, before the first AI use on a real decision)
1. What's my read, in one line, and how sure am I (0–100%)? "I don't know yet" counts.
2. What do I know here that AI can't: about this person, this room, my own history with this? *And what might AI see that I can't?*
3. After AI: where exactly do we disagree, and what would settle it? What do I change, and what do I keep?
4. What am I putting my name on, and who needs to hear it from me in person?

### Rotating (one a day, 30 seconds)
5. **Energy:** how did I sleep, and how's my energy, 1 to 5? If it's low, which decision gets a second look tomorrow? (A rule of thumb: tired people take more risks and have less self-control. There's no magic number of hours.)
6. **People:** who did I have a real conversation with today? Did I take something to AI that I'd normally take to a person? Who will tell me when I'm wrong?
7. **Meaning:** what did I do today that mattered, and which part was mine before AI touched it?

### For coaches before a client session
8. What's my read on this client right now, before I look at any notes or AI summary?
9. What am I avoiding saying to them? What would I say if I knew they'd thank me later?
10. What will I only notice if I'm fully present in the session?

### Weekly review (10 minutes, ideally Monday morning)
11. Look back at last week's reads. Only score the ones where the outcome is now known; give each read a "check-by" date when you write it.
12. Score the reasoning separately from the result. A good call can turn out badly through luck, and a bad call can get lucky.
13. Where was I over-sure? Where did AI beat me? Where did I beat it? What one pattern do I see?
14. Send a three-line summary to one person: a peer, a coach, a partner.

## Set-up (so it lasts past week two)

Most people quit habit apps within the first one to two weeks. Plan for that up front:

- **One if-then cue,** tied to a moment that already happens: "If I open AI for a real decision, then I write my read first." Have them say it back in their own words.
- **A miss plan:** "If I skip a day, the next day I do the one-line version." Missing a day doesn't break a habit; quitting after missing one does.
- **Count total entries, not streaks.** Never tell someone their streak is broken. Celebrate coming back after a gap: in a study of 54 gym programmes, rewarding people for returning after a miss worked best.
- **One accountability person,** who gets the weekly three-line summary. Self-monitoring works better when it's shared.
- **Fresh starts:** if they've drifted, restart on a Monday or the 1st of the month.
- **Where to keep it:** wherever they already write: a notes app, a paper notebook, a spreadsheet, a pinned chat. The best log is the one they'll open.
- **Measure it simply:** entries per week, the share of real decisions with a read written first, and after about eight weeks, how often their confident reads turned out right.

The two-minute default is practitioner advice, not a research finding. Keep it small so it survives busy days.

## Privacy and wellbeing

- Don't put confidential client or customer details into AI tools or shared logs. Write "Client A" instead of names.
- This is a thinking habit, not health or mental-health care. If a question brings up something heavy, talk to someone you trust or a professional.
