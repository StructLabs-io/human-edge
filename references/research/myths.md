# Myths and overclaims: what to stop repeating, and what to say instead

Use this before a talk, a post, a pitch or a client session. Coaches and consultants repeat these often. Each one fails a check against the original source.

## Goal and habit myths

| Claim | What's actually true | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "You're 95% likely to succeed with a specific accountability appointment" (credited to ASTD/ATD) | No primary source exists: ATD's own pages lack it, and no study behind it could be found (searched October 2026) | "Tracking your progress helps, and it helps more when you report it to someone" ([Harkin et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000025): d = 0.40, larger when shared) |
| The Yale (1953) or Harvard (1979) study: "the 3% with written goals earned 10 times more" | The study never happened: no such Yale study exists ([Fast Company, 1996](https://www.fastcompany.com/27953/if-your-goal-success-dont-consult-these-gurus)), and the Harvard version is equally untraceable ([Matthews](https://scholar.dominican.edu/psychology-faculty-conference-presentations/3/)) | Cite Harkin (above) or say nothing |
| Gail Matthews' Dominican University written-goals study | Real, but weak: a 2007 conference presentation, not a peer-reviewed paper; 149 of 267 recruited finished; self-rated outcomes ([source](https://scholar.dominican.edu/psychology-faculty-conference-presentations/3/)) | Use only with those caveats |
| "21 days to form a habit" | Contradicted: 18 to 254 days in the main study ([Lally et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)) | "Simple habits took about two months for people who formed them; complex ones vary a lot" |
| "66 days to form a habit" as a rule | It's a median from 39 people | Same as above |
| "Most people quit at a month" | Most quit in the first one to two weeks ([Baumel et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.2196/14567); [Medito study, 2026](https://doi.org/10.2196/79366)) | "Plan for week two" |
| Implementation intentions "d = 0.65" | An older, smaller meta-analysis. The 2024 update of 642 tests found d = .27 to .66 depending on the outcome ([Sheeran et al., 2024](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/69905)) | "If-then plans help, more for some outcomes than others" |
| "Premortems improve accuracy by 30%" | The study counted the *reasons* people generated, not accuracy ([Mitchell, Russo & Pennington, 1989](https://doi.org/10.1002/bdm.3960020103)) | "Imagining failure helps people think of more reasons it could fail" |
| "Gratitude journaling boosts performance" | About zero against active controls ([Davis et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/cou0000107)) | Offer it as optional, with no promised effect |

## AI myths

| Claim | What's actually true | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "ChatGPT rots your brain" or "causes brain damage" | A small preprint measured brain connectivity during a writing task; the authors' FAQ asks people not to say "brain rot", "harm" or "damage" ([FAQ](https://www.brainonllm.com/faq); [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872)) | "In one small study, people who let ChatGPT write their essays remembered less of what they'd written and felt less ownership of it" |
| "AI destroys critical thinking" | The two most-cited sources are correlational self-report surveys ([Gerlich, 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/soc15010006), later corrected; [Lee et al., 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/)) | "People who trust AI more report thinking less critically about its output" |
| "AI makes developers 19% slower" | 16 developers, early-2025 tools; the same lab's 2026 update points the other way ([METR, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089); [update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)) | "In one small study, developers believed AI sped them up even when it slowed them down" |
| "Doctors deskill in three months" | One observational study ([Budzyń et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-1253%2825%2900133-5)); a 2026 prospective trial found none ([Pedersen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2858-7084)) | "The evidence on deskilling in medicine is mixed" |
| "80% of people accept wrong AI answers" | That rate is for trick puzzles, on trials where people chose to consult the AI, in a preprint ([Shaw & Nave, 2026](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=6097646)) | "When people consulted AI on trick questions, they mostly followed it, even when it was wrong" |
| "Empathy and creativity are uniquely human" | Not as written outputs: AI out-scores most people on rated compassion and average creativity (`ai-already-matches.md`) | "AI can write caring words. It can't be the person who chose to show up" |
| "AI beats superforecasters" | "Likely parity", extrapolated, and disputed ([FRI, 2026](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity); [Good Judgment](https://goodjudgment.com/what-forecastbench-doesnt-measure/)) | "AI forecasting may now match the best human forecasters" |
| "People felt twice as cared for by a human" | The rating gap in the main study is about half a point on a 10-point scale ([Rubin et al., 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02247-w)) | "Almost half would rather wait for a human reply, some for up to two years" |
| "Experience is gaining value" | Experienced workers' *employment* is holding up, descriptively; there's no evidence their pay is rising ([Canaries, 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf)) | "Experienced workers are holding their ground while entry-level hiring in AI-exposed jobs falls" |
| "Clients pay more for humans" | Most evidence is stated preference; interpersonal skills are "not currently prioritized in wage-based evaluations" ([Shao et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06576)) | "Clients say they still want a person for..." |
| "Thinking first is proven to protect your skills" | Two 2026 trials support it, neither preregistered; a two-week follow-up found no lasting difference ([Yan, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1936522/full)) | "Committing to your own view first cut copying of bad AI advice in one study" |
| "Human plus AI always beats either alone" | On average, human–AI teams did worse than the better of the two on decision tasks ([Vaccaro et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-02024-1)) | "It depends on who's stronger at the task" |

## Habits of honest citation

- **Say "one study found" when it's one study.** Name the year and, if it helps, the journal.
- **Separate stated preference from behaviour.** "64% said they'd prefer" is not "64% left".
- **Flag vendor data.** Studies from companies that sell AI or advisory services (Anthropic, Microsoft, Salesforce, BetterUp, Gartner) can be good, but say who published them.
- **Check the denominator.** "18.9% still active after two weeks" in the Medito study is a share of the 655 users analysed, not of everyone who downloaded the app. Say what the percentage is a percentage *of*.
