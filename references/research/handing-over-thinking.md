# What happens when people hand over the thinking

Studies are listed most recent first within each theme. N = sample size. A *preprint* has not been peer-reviewed yet.

The summary line: **how you use AI matters more than whether you use it.** Letting AI do the core work while learning erodes the skill, and wrong AI pulls judgment off course in the moment, experts included. Using AI as a tutor or critic mostly protected learning. Getting a second opinion from AI still needs a check of your own (section 2).

## 1. Skill and learning: offloading during practice lowers later unaided performance

| Study | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| [Maier, Schwabe, Schneider & Feuerriegel, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20143), "Designing Against Deskilling" (preprint) | Preregistered RCT, N = 704, practice with on-request AI | Metacognitive feedback cut answer offloading (odds ratio 0.47) and raised later unaided performance (OR 1.51). AI access by itself did not impair unaided performance |
| [Contractor & Reyes, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08849), "Experimental Evidence on the Learning Impact of Generative AI" (preprint) | Registered, proctored RCT, 211 undergraduates | AI access raised test scores 0.28 SD immediately and 0.21 SD a week later (p = .068). Students who had AI write for them lost their essay advantage a week later. Tutor-style users kept a small gain (use type was self-chosen, so that split is descriptive) |
| [Strömberg, Lei & Wu, 2026](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f240545.pdf), "The Generative AI Learning Penalty" (working paper) | Difference-in-differences, 26,811 Chinese students, grades 7–12, 30 months; not randomized | Homework scores +18% and completion time −30%, but monthly exams −20% within six months and entrance exams −18% and −24%. Losses sat among the ~80% whose behaviour looked like outsourcing |
| [Rismanchian et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21629), "Faster Completion, Less Learning" (preprint) | 3.2M ALEKS interactions, 12.2M placement responses | Learning time on AI-susceptible problems fell 26.9%; proctored retention odds fell 25%. Authors mostly McGraw Hill staff; AI use inferred |
| [Shen & Tamkin, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20245), "How AI Impacts Skill Formation" ([Anthropic post](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills); *vendor*) | RCT, n = 52, mostly junior developers learning a new library | AI group averaged 50% on the quiz vs 67% hand-coding: 17 points, about 25% lower. Delegation patterns averaged below 40%; conceptual-question patterns 65% or higher. No significant speed gain |
| [Bastani et al., *PNAS*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122), "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning" ([Wharton summary](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education/)) | Field RCT, ~1,000 high-school maths students in Turkey | Practice grades +48% (plain GPT) and +127% (GPT tutor). On the unaided exam, plain-GPT students scored 17% below controls; the tutor version "largely mitigated" the harm |
| [Kumar et al., CHI 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3714198), "Human Creativity in the Age of LLMs" | Two preregistered experiments, N = 1,100 | LLM help raised creativity during assisted tasks but "may inadvertently hinder" it once people worked alone |
| [Anthropic Education Report, 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-education-report-how-university-students-use-claude) (*vendor*) | 574,740 conversations, filtered from about 1 million on university-linked accounts | Describes how students use Claude, including direct requests for answers |
| [Fütterer et al., *Educational Psychology Review*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10133-8) | RCT, N = 371 school students (grades 7–9) | Tested two generative-AI support designs for self-regulated learning against a standard-ChatGPT control |
| [Kestin et al., *Scientific Reports*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6) | RCT | A carefully designed AI tutor produced more learning than in-class active learning |

**How to say it:** "In studies of people learning something new, those who let AI do the core work scored roughly a fifth lower once AI was removed." The figure is a range across studies with different measures (relative grades, percentage points, exam scores), not a pooled estimate. And it is scoped to *learning*.

## 2. Judgment: wrong AI pulls people, including experts, below their unaided level

| Study | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| [Surdilla et al., *AJHP*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajhp/zxag259) | Randomized simulation, 77 pharmacists | Accuracy 80.2% with correct AI, 26.1% with incorrect AI. They accepted 73.9% of incorrect recommendations; when they overrode the AI they were right only 21.7% of the time |
| [Marcoccia, Quattrociocchi & Capraro, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13562) (preprint) | 5 experiments (4 preregistered), N = 3,132; AI advice deliberately wrong | AI access "nearly eliminated participants' willingness to suspend judgment". They answered more, were right about a third as often, and their confidence nearly doubled. Accuracy incentives helped but did not restore "I don't know" |
| [Hayden et al., *BMC Medical Education*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09979-x) | 185 medical students ranked their own diagnoses before and after AI | AI raised accuracy, but students "did not appear to reliably distinguish helpful from harmful AI influence", even after committing first |
| [Taib et al., *Radiology*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.252590) | 10 NHS mammogram readers, 60 cases, eye tracking | On cancers the AI missed, sensitivity was 39% with AI vs 71% unaided; readers looked less at those cases |
| [Ratajczyk et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/83jum_v1), "Thinking With AI, Reasoning Without It: Cognitive Offloading to Generative AI Weakens Effort Regulation" (preprint) | Preregistered lab study, N = 193 | Offloading reasoning to generative AI weakened how people regulated their own effort |
| [Shaw & Nave, 2026](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=6097646), "Thinking: Fast, Slow, and Artificial" (preprint) | 3 preregistered experiments, N = 1,372, adapted trick puzzles | Accuracy +25 points with correct AI, −15 with faulty AI. People followed faulty AI on 79.8% of the trials where they consulted it; incentives plus feedback cut this to 57.9%. Coins "cognitive surrender" |
| [Dratsch et al., *Radiology*, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.222176) | 27 radiologists, mammograms | Inexperienced readers were 79.7% correct when the AI suggested the right category and 19.8% when it suggested a wrong one; very experienced readers, 82.3% vs 45.5% |
| [Parasuraman & Manzey, *Human Factors*, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720810376055) | Review | Automation bias affects novices and experts and "cannot be prevented by training or instructions" |

## 3. Confidence comes apart from performance

- **Developers misjudged their speed.** [METR, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089): 16 experienced developers, 246 tasks, early-2025 tools. AI made them 19% slower. They had forecast 24% faster, and afterwards believed they'd been 20% faster. [METR's 2026 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) says developers are likely faster with current tools, with heavy selection caveats. Use this as a calibration finding, never as "AI makes you slower".
- **Confidence in AI went with less critical thinking.** (*vendor*: Microsoft) [Lee et al., Microsoft Research and CMU, CHI 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/): 319 knowledge workers, 936 examples. Higher confidence in AI went with less critical thinking; higher self-confidence went with more. AI shifts critical thinking toward verifying, integrating and stewarding responses. Self-report.
- **AI literacy didn't fix self-assessment.** [Fernandes et al., *Computers in Human Behavior*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2025.108779): N = 246 plus a 452-person replication, logic test items. AI raised scores about 3 points, but people overestimated by about 4. Higher AI literacy went with *worse* self-assessment.
- **Few users check.** [Anthropic AI Fluency Index, 2026](https://academy.claude.com/tutorials/the-ai-fluency-index) (*vendor*): 9,830 conversations. Only 15.8% questioned Claude's reasoning and 8.7% checked facts. Checking fell further when Claude produced a finished artifact. Correlational.

## 4. Memory, ownership and meaning

- **Ownership and meaning.** [Lee, Yin, Jia & Wakslak, *Scientific Reports*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-42312-6) ([Penn State release](https://www.psu.edu/news/research/story/passive-ai-use-work-increases-feelings-work-meaninglessness-study-finds)): about 270 professionals.
  - Passive AI use cut ownership by nearly 20%, and meaning and self-efficacy by nearly 10%.
  - The drops in meaning and self-efficacy persisted after people returned to working alone.
  - Using AI to "workshop their own ideas" did not differ meaningfully from working unaided.
  - The paper itself couldn't be read in full; these figures come from the university release.
- **Motivation after switching back.** [Wu et al., *Scientific Reports*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-98385-2): 4 preregistered experiments, N = 3,562. AI collaboration improved the assisted task but not later solo work. Switching from AI to solo work lowered intrinsic motivation (5.08 to 4.39 on 7 points) and raised boredom.
- **Timing.** [Qin et al., "Timing Matters", CHI 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713146) (on AI-assisted ideation): N = 60. Using an LLM from the start reduced original ideas, creative self-efficacy and self-credit. The authors "propose delaying the use of LLMs".
- **Memory.** [Kosmyna et al., "Your Brain on ChatGPT", 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872) (preprint): 54 participants, 18 in the final session.
  - LLM users showed the weakest brain connectivity, struggled to quote their own essays, and reported the lowest ownership.
  - People who wrote unaided first and then used the LLM showed higher recall.
  - A formal critique raises sample, EEG-method and reporting problems ([Stanković et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2601.00856)).
  - The authors' [FAQ](https://www.brainonllm.com/faq) asks people not to describe it as "brain rot", "harm" or "damage". Cite only the ownership and recall findings.
- **Writing.** [Chen, *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1906199) looks at which layers of the writing process people offload to AI.

## 5. Ideas and voice become more alike

- **Homogenisation review.** [Sourati, Ziabari & Dehghani, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2026.01.003): LLMs homogenise human style and reasoning.
- **Shallower learning.** [Melumad & Yun, *PNAS Nexus*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf316): 7 experiments, n = 10,462. Learning from LLM summaries produced shallower knowledge and "sparser, less original" advice than web search.
- **Western style.** [Agarwal, Naaman & Vashistha, CHI 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713564): N = 118. AI suggestions pushed Indian writers toward Western styles. Relevant for Malaysian and other Asian writers.
- **Better but more similar.** [Doshi & Hauser, *Science Advances*, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290): AI ideas raised individual novelty (+8.1%) and usefulness (+9.0%), most for the least creative writers, but the stories became "more similar to each other".
- **Group brainstorming.** [Meincke, Nave & Terwiesch, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02173-x): ChatGPT narrowed the diversity of ideas a group produced (a short published comment on [Lee & Chung, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-01953-1)).
- **Opinions shift.** [Jakesch et al., CHI 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581196): N = 1,506. Writing with an opinionated model shifted both the opinions people wrote and their own views.

## 6. Counter-evidence: where erosion did not appear

- **Colonoscopy: no deskilling in a trial.** [Pedersen et al., *Endoscopy*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2858-7084): prospective trial, 13 endoscopists, 5,013 colonoscopies. After the AI was removed, "no upskilling or deskilling was observed".
- **Colonoscopy: a drop in an observational study.** [Budzyń et al., *Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-1253%2825%2900133-5): adenoma detection fell from 28.4% to 22.4% after AI exposure, in a before-and-after design.
- **Access alone didn't harm.** In Maier et al. and Contractor & Reyes (above), how people used AI decided the outcome.
- **Never-skilling is still a hypothesis.** [Ke et al., *Nature Medicine*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04438-y) note that "direct evidence from medical training is absent", and propose building AI-free baseline skill first. A framework, not data.

## 7. Supported vs overclaimed

**Supported:**
- Letting AI do the core work while learning lowers later unaided performance, by roughly a fifth.
- Wrong AI output degrades judgment, including expert judgment, and training alone doesn't prevent it.
- People misjudge how much AI helps them and how well they did.
- AI-assisted writing narrows ideas and voice, and passive use drains meaning.

**Overclaimed in the media:**
- **"ChatGPT rots your brain."** The MIT team's own FAQ asks people not to say "brain rot", "harm" or "damage". Brain connectivity during a task is not lasting harm.
- **"AI makes developers 19% slower."** Sixteen developers, early-2025 tools; METR's 2026 update points the other way.
- **"AI destroys critical thinking."** The two most-cited sources are correlational self-report surveys: [Gerlich, 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/soc15010006) (which issued a correction) and Lee et al., 2025.
- **"Doctors deskill in three months."** One observational study; a 2026 prospective trial found none.
- **"80% accept wrong AI answers."** That rate is for trick puzzles, on trials where people chose to consult the AI, in a preprint.
