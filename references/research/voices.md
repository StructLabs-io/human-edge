## Frameworks

- **MIT Sloan EPOCH** ([Loaiza & Rigobon, 2025](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/these-human-capabilities-complement-ais-shortcomings); [paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=5028371)). Five groups of human capabilities:
  1. Empathy and emotional intelligence
  2. Presence, networking and connectedness
  3. Opinion, judgment and ethics
  4. Creativity and imagination
  5. Hope, vision and leadership

  All five were linked to employment growth, Hope most strongly and Opinion second. US occupational data; correlational.
- **Mollick's "Overhang"** ([2026](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-overhang)). Four advantages: deep knowledge, wide knowledge, taste and agency.
- **Microsoft Work Trend Index 2025** ([2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born); *vendor*). Three human skills: "creativity, judgment, and connection-building".
- **Godin's list** ([2026](https://seths.blog/2026/05/in-search-of-ai/)). 17 contributions, most of them sub-skills of judgment, care and initiative.

## What the voices actually converge on

Across the voices and frameworks, the most-named items are:
- **Judgment:** Hassabis, Mollick, Kahneman, EPOCH, Microsoft.
- **Taste:** Katzenberg, Mollick, Hassabis.
- **Creativity or voice:** Katzenberg, Chiang, EPOCH, Microsoft.
- **Empathy or connection:** Turkle, Perel, Brown, EPOCH, Microsoft.

"Owning the call" is named mostly by Godin, plus the law. "Telling someone what they don't want to hear" is named by no one in this list; it comes from the evidence that AI flatters by default (Cheng et al., 2026).

So there's a gap. What people *name* leans toward judgment, taste and creativity. What *survives the evidence check* best is a person who shows up, owns the call, has lived it and will tell you the truth. When you present this, say "they named a lot of things; when I checked the research, a few held up", not "they all said the same thing".
