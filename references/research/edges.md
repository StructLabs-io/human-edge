# The edges in detail

Every study behind the five edges in `../findings.md`, with numbers and links. Evidence labels: **Strong**, **Moderate**, **Preliminary**, **Weak**, **Contested** (defined in `../findings.md`). Vendor data (from companies that sell AI or AI services) is marked *vendor*.

## How the list was built

About 25 credible voices and five published frameworks name dozens of "human skills". Once synonyms merge, they come to 12 named edges, plus one meta-edge: calibrated trust in AI. An audit then pointed out that several overlap and mix different kinds of things (capabilities, roles, states). Merging by what they share gives about five clusters:
- MIT Sloan's EPOCH framework has five ([Loaiza & Rigobon, 2025](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/these-human-capabilities-complement-ais-shortcomings)).
- Mollick's "Overhang" has four ([2026](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-overhang)).
- Microsoft's 2025 Work Trend Index names three: "creativity, judgment, and connection-building" ([2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born)).

How the 12 named edges and the meta-edge map to the five used in this kit:

| Named edge | Cluster in this kit |
|---|---|
| Provenance (a person chose to show up), relationships and trust over time, embodied presence | 1. Relational presence |
| Accountability and ownership | 2. Accountability |
| Field-tested tacit expertise, your own voice and lived experience | 3. Lived experience |
| Contextual judgment, knowing what you don't know, problem-finding, taste | 4. Judgment |
| Unwelcome honesty | 5. Honest challenge |
| Meaning and purpose, agency and hope | Weaker or contested (below) |

"About a dozen" is fair as a count of named items, not of distinct edges. A list of twenty "human skills" pads synonyms and includes things AI already matches.

## Edge 1: Relational presence: a person who shows up

Being there for someone: face to face, on a call, over time. Being the one who chose to spend scarce attention on them. The value sits partly outside the words: in knowing a person chose to show up.

**The label effect: same words, different value**
- [Rubin et al., *Nature Human Behaviour*, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02247-w) (9 studies, N = 6,282). Every reply was written by AI; only the label ("human" or "AI") changed. Replies labelled human were rated more empathic. The penalty was strongest for emotional sharing and care. *Strong, for emotional support.*
  - **Who would wait for a human.** In study 4 (preregistered), people shared an emotional experience, then chose between an instant AI reply and waiting a set time for a human one: 2 hours, 24 hours, 1 week, 2 months or 2 years. 45% (219 of 489) chose to wait. When the wait offered was two years, 43% (42 of 98) still chose to wait. The paper reports that the length of the wait made no significant difference (p = .46).
  - **Just to be read.** In study 5, 33% (162 of 486) chose to wait just to have a human *read* their story, with no reply at all.
  - **Size of the rating gap.** It is real but small: 7.66 vs 7.14 on a 0–9 scale (study 1a; Cohen's d ≈ 0.34). Don't say "twice as cared for".
  - **Where the numbers come from.** The design, the sample of 489 and the "no effect of wait length" result are in the paper and in the authors' post-review manuscript ([OSF preprint](https://doi.org/10.31219/osf.io/ng97s_v2)). The 45%, 43% and 33% figures were counted for this kit from the authors' public data ([OSF](https://osf.io/w4hkd/)); the manuscript doesn't state them in its text. The published paper is the authority if they differ.
- [Yin, Jia & Wakslak, *PNAS*, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319112121). AI-written replies made people feel *more* heard (+0.57), and the AI label cost about the same (−0.68).
- [Reis et al., *Nature Medicine*, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03180-7) (N = 2,280). Medical advice labelled "human + AI" was rated lower, even with a doctor supervising.
- [Schilke & Reimann, *OBHDP*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2025.104405) (13 experiments). Disclosing AI use cost some professional trust; being found out by someone else cost more.

**In business and coaching**
- [Gartner, 2024](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-07-09-gartner-survey-finds-64-percent-of-customers-would-prefer-that-companies-didnt-use-ai-for-customer-service) (N = 5,728, fielded December 2023). 64% of customers said they'd prefer companies not use AI in customer service; 53% would consider switching to a competitor. Their top fear: it gets harder to reach a person. *Preliminary: stated preference; vendor survey.*
- [Palmatier, Scheer & Steenkamp, *Journal of Marketing Research*, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.44.2.185) (362 B2B buyer–salesperson pairs). Loyalty to the *salesperson*, not to the firm, directly drove sales growth. *Moderate: one B2B study, 2007.*
- [de Haan, Terblanche & Nowack, *HRDI*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/13678868.2026.2633990) (N = 114 managers). Managers with human coaches improved on goal attainment and stress; the AI chatbot group "did not improve significantly on any of the outcome variables". The bot was a scripted chatbot, not a frontier model, and the AI arm had high dropout. *Preliminary.* (A working-alliance effect size of d = 1.95 circulates for this study; it could not be confirmed, so don't quote it.)
- [Passmore et al., *JWAM*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1108/JWAM-02-2025-0032) (n = 63, not randomized). Human coaches scored higher on insight, goal attainment, commitment and alliance; no difference on trust or confidentiality. *Preliminary.*

**Being in the room**
- (*vendor*) [Tomlinson et al., Microsoft Research, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07935). The work activities with the lowest AI applicability involve physically working with people. *Moderate, mostly by exclusion.*
- [Sherry Turkle, 2025](https://www.afterbabel.com/p/reclaiming-conversation-age-of-ai): real empathy commits to staying the course; a machine has no stake in your troubles.

**Caveats that matter**
- **The premium shrinks.** In six countries, the share preferring a human fell by about 11 points once people learned about the algorithm, and lower still after good experiences with algorithms ([Kaiser, Sunstein & Reisch, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11166-025-09466-6)).
- **AI still won on compassion in one study even with the source disclosed** ([Ovsyannikova et al., 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-024-00182-6)).
- **People sometimes prefer algorithms.** For many judgments, people weight algorithmic advice *more* than human advice ([Logg, Minson & Moore, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2018.12.005)). The counterweight is "algorithm aversion" after seeing an algorithm err ([Dietvorst et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000033)).
- **Implication:** treat presence as a premium you earn by showing up, not a permanent moat. And hiding AI use is the worst option. Say what AI did and what you did.

## Edge 2: Accountability: putting your name on the call

Someone answers for the outcome and can be held to it.

- **Law and regulation**
  - The EU AI Act (Art. 14) requires high-risk AI to be "effectively overseen by natural persons" with power to override or stop it ([text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/)).
  - Malaysia's National Guidelines on AI Governance and Ethics (September 2024, voluntary) make accountability Principle 6 and human oversight Principle 7 ([AIGE](https://www.malaysia.gov.my/en/my-initiative/whole-government-digital-services/tadbir-urus/national-guidelines-on-artificial-intelligence-governance-and-ethics-aige)). No binding Malaysian AI law exists yet.
  - The American Bar Association's Formal Opinion 512 bars lawyers relying on AI output without appropriate independent review ([ABA, 2024](https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba/administrative/professional_responsibility/ethics-opinions/aba-formal-opinion-512.pdf)).
- **A tribunal held the company responsible.** A tribunal rejected Air Canada's claim that its chatbot was a separate entity responsible for its own words ([Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024](https://decisions.civilresolutionbc.ca/crt/crtd/en/item/525448/index.do)). Separately, by October 2026 a public database had logged 2,184 court cases where lawyers or litigants filed AI-invented content ([Charlotin](https://www.damiencharlotin.com/hallucinations/)).
- **What customers and workers say**
  - 45% are more likely to use an AI agent that has a clear path to a human ([Salesforce, 2024](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/ai-customer-research/), N = 15,015; *vendor*).
  - Only 18% of consumers would trust AI alone for financial recommendations ([TD Bank, 2026](https://stories.td.com/us/en/article/nearly-80-of-americans-use-ai-tools-but-most-still-want-humans-making-financial-decisions-td-survey-finds), N = 2,504; *industry survey*).
  - 86% of AI-using knowledge workers said they treat AI output as a starting point and "stay responsible for the thinking" ([Microsoft Work Trend Index, 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization); self-report; *vendor*; Malaysia not sampled).
- **Agents still need a person behind them.** An AI agent left to run a small shop lost money in its first phase; in the second it turned a profit but staff manipulated it and it "still needed a great deal of human support" ([Anthropic, Project Vend, 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-1); [phase 2](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2); *vendor*).
- **Inside the user, too.** Passive AI use cut people's felt ownership of their work by nearly 20% ([Lee, Yin, Jia & Wakslak, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-42312-6)). Felt ownership is a different thing from being accountable, but it's how the habit of owning the call wears down.
- **Grade:** *Strong as a structural requirement in some places; moderate evidence that people value it.*

## Edge 3: Lived and field-tested experience

Pattern knowledge from years in a domain ("we can know more than we can tell"), first-hand information in no dataset (this customer, this supplier, this conversation), and your own voice.

**For**
- **Experienced workers are holding their ground.** Employment of 22- to 25-year-olds in AI-exposed jobs is 19% lower than it would have been had it kept pace with less-exposed peers, while experienced workers show "no comparable gap" ([Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, Stanford, 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf)). The authors call this descriptive, not causal. The pattern weakens when controlling for education, and the adjustment runs through employment, not pay.
- **Domain command went with more success.** In about 400,000 Claude Code sessions, people with domain command succeeded at about twice the novice rate: 28–33% vs about 15% ([Anthropic, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-code-expertise); *vendor*). Expertise was inferred by a model from the same transcripts, and the study is correlational.
- **People still make the plans.** In the same data, people made about 70% of planning decisions and Claude about 80% of execution decisions.
- **Experts keep the hard part.** Hard tasks are "based on intuition, experience and judgment" ([Acemoglu, NBER, 2024](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32487)). AI could help rebuild middle-skill expertise ([Autor, 2024](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32140); [Autor & Thompson, 2025](https://blueprintlabs.mit.edu/research/expertise)).
- **Your own voice.** Katzenberg: AI doesn't yet have "the kind of creativity that comes from a person trying to say something only they could say" ([2026](https://x.com/jeffreykWNDR/status/2102774995315245469)). Ted Chiang: art comes from many choices made with an intention to communicate ([*New Yorker*, 2024](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/the-weekend-essay/why-ai-isnt-going-to-make-art)).
- **Intuition comes from experience.** Gary Klein: intuitions come from tacit knowledge built through experience ([Edge](https://www.edge.org/conversation/gary_klein-insight)).

**Against**
- **AI narrows the novice–veteran gap.** It raised support agents' productivity 14% on average and 34% for novices, with little gain for veterans ([Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, *QJE*, 2025](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161)). AI spreads expertise as well as rewarding it.
- **Juniors may never build it.** If they use AI for the core of their work, they may never build the skill: "never-skilling" ([Ke et al., *Nature Medicine*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04438-y)). That is a framework; direct evidence from training is still missing.

**Grade:** *Moderate.*

## Edge 4: Judgment, including knowing what you don't know

Deciding what fits *this* person, place and set of values when there's no single right answer; knowing when to rely on AI and when to cross-check.

**The weakness is well documented** (*Strong*). When AI is wrong, people follow it, and experts are not immune. The detail is in `handing-over-thinking.md`:
- **Radiologists:** inexperienced readers were 79.7% correct when the AI suggested the right category and 19.8% when it suggested a wrong one; very experienced readers, 82.3% vs 45.5% ([Dratsch et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.222176)).
- **Pharmacists:** accuracy was 80.2% with correct AI and 26.1% with incorrect AI ([Surdilla et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajhp/zxag259)).
- **Training doesn't fix it.** Automation bias affects novices and experts and "cannot be prevented by training or instructions" ([Parasuraman & Manzey, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720810376055)).

**"I don't know" disappears** (*Preliminary*). Access to AI advice nearly eliminated people's willingness to suspend judgment. They answered more, were right about a third as often, and their confidence nearly doubled ([Marcoccia, Quattrociocchi & Capraro, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13562); 5 experiments, N = 3,132; preprint; the AI advice was deliberately wrong).
- **"Cognitive surrender".** People followed faulty AI on about 80% of the trials where they consulted it ([Shaw & Nave, 2026](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=6097646); preprint; trick puzzles).
- **"Extended cognitive hygiene".** Andy Clark argues we need to become better estimators of what to trust and when ([*Nature Communications*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59906-9)).

**Knowing where the frontier is *is* the skill.**
- **The jagged frontier.** Consultants using AI did better and faster on tasks inside AI's abilities, and were markedly less likely to be correct on tasks just outside them ([Dell'Acqua et al., *Organization Science*, 2026; working paper 2023](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838)).
- **It goes beyond task skill.** Users said AI "lacks the judgment, contextual awareness, and situational reasoning that their work requires" ([Anthropic survey of about 9,700 users, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report); *vendor*).

**Contested: is judgment human-only?**
- **Forecasting.** AI has "likely reached parity" with superforecasters ([Forecasting Research Institute, 2026](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity)), though the comparison is extrapolated, and [Good Judgment disputes it](https://goodjudgment.com/what-forecastbench-doesnt-measure/).
- **Perceived moral advice.** GPT-4o was rated more moral and trustworthy than the *New York Times* Ethicist ([Dillion et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-86510-0)).
- **The counter-argument.** No intrinsic barrier stops AI learning judgment; it comes from feedback and experience ([Steven Adler, 2026](https://www.clear-eyed.ai/p/judgment-isnt-uniquely-human)).
- **The other side.** Taste or judgment will be among the hardest things for AI to mimic ([Demis Hassabis, 2025](https://lexfridman.com/demis-hassabis-2-transcript/)).
- **Noise.** Wherever there is judgment, there is noise, and simple remedies reduce it ([Kahneman, Sibony & Sunstein, 2021](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/daniel-kahneman/noise/9780316451406/)).

**Grade:** *Strong as a vulnerability; contested as a human-only capability.* Don't sell "judgment" as untouchable. Sell the habit of checking.

## Edge 5: Honest challenge

Telling someone what they need to hear, including that they're wrong.

- **AI flatters by default.** Across 11 models, AI affirmed users about 49% more often than humans did, including when the user described deception or harm ([Cheng et al., *Science*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec8352); 3 preregistered experiments, N = 2,405; [coverage](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/28/stanford-study-outlines-dangers-of-asking-ai-chatbots-for-personal-advice/)). One interaction made people less willing to repair a conflict, and users trusted and preferred the flattering models. *Strong.*
- **Persuasion costs accuracy.** More persuasive models were less accurate ([Hackenburg, Tappin et al., *Science*, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13919)).
- **Being preferred isn't the same as being good for someone.** The useful human edge is willingness to say the unwelcome thing.
- **Malaysia and much of Asia.** In high power-distance, face-conscious cultures, honest challenge is harder to exercise and has to be delivered with care. Malaysia scores 100 on The Culture Factor's version of Hofstede's power-distance index, among the highest of any country ([The Culture Factor](https://www.theculturefactor.com/country-comparison-tool?countries=malaysia)). In our judgment, that makes it rarer, not less valuable.
- **No voice names it.** None of the founders and writers in `voices.md` names this edge directly. It is the edge the evidence most clearly points to, because AI's default is the opposite.

## Weaker or contested, but often named

| Edge | Evidence | Key sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Meaning and purpose:** knowing why the work matters | Moderate, mostly as something AI use can erode | Passive AI use lowered felt meaning ([Lee et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-42312-6)); switching from AI to solo work lowered intrinsic motivation from 5.08 to 4.39 on a 7-point scale ([Wu et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-98385-2)); AI is "a how-to engine, not a why engine" ([Arthur Brooks, 2026](https://conversationswithtyler.com/episodes/arthur-brooks/)) |
| **Problem-finding:** choosing which problem to solve and what to ask | Weak to moderate (practitioner voices) | [Godin, 2026](https://seths.blog/2026/05/in-search-of-ai/) (sitting with the right problem); [Mollick, 2026](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/management-as-ai-superpower) (what's scarce is knowing what to ask for) |
| **Agency, initiative and hope:** acting without being told; setting a vision | Weak to moderate | In EPOCH, "Hope, vision and leadership" showed the largest link to employment growth ([MIT Sloan, 2025](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/these-human-capabilities-complement-ais-shortcomings); correlational, US data); if agents take every interesting decision, we automate the wrong half of the job ([Mollick, 2026](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/agency-and-agents)) |
| **Taste:** knowing what good looks like and choosing among options | Contested (strong practitioner consensus, no rigorous study) | "The scarce resource is your ability to select among stuff using your own taste" ([Mollick, 2026](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-overhang)); lay readers rated AI poems higher and spotted them below chance, 46.6% ([Porter & Machery, 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-76900-1)) |

## Edges worth adding for small and medium businesses

The audit found these missing from most published lists, and they matter to owner-run businesses in Malaysia and the region:
- **First-hand private information.** What you know that is in no dataset: this customer, this supplier, the conversation you had last week.
- **Relationship capital and networks.** Introductions, reputation, who will take your call. (EPOCH's "networking" is close to this.)
- **Physical craft and skilled operations.** The work AI is least applicable to ([Tomlinson et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07935)). Relevant to F&B, manufacturing, construction and trades.
- **Local and cultural fluency.** Code-switching between languages, local norms, face. AI suggestions pushed Indian writers toward Western styles ([Agarwal, Naaman & Vashistha, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713564)).

## Also folded in (not separate edges)

- **Empathy and listening to what isn't said.** The *wording* of empathy is matched by AI (see `ai-already-matches.md`). What holds lives in Edge 1: a person chose to show up, committed to stay, and is reading the room in person.
- **Moral reasoning.** Moral *advice* is matched. Moral *agency* (choosing your values and standing behind them) sits in Edges 2 and 5 and in meaning.
- **Courage** sits in Edge 5 (saying the unwelcome thing) and in agency (acting under uncertainty).
