# What helps: practices that protect or strengthen the edges

The evidence behind the think-first loop in `../sharpen-your-edge.md`, and what keeps a reflection habit alive. Each step of the loop is drawn from research on that step. **The loop as a whole hasn't been tested as a package.**

## 1. Practices, graded

| Practice | How it works | Evidence | Key sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Write your read before AI** | Commit before seeing advice; generate rather than recognise | Moderate (several RCTs; mixed on overall accuracy) | [Yan, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1936522/full); [Liu et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1910545) |
| Generation, retrieval, pretesting | Producing an answer, even a wrong one, makes later learning stick | Strong | Testing effect g = 0.50 across 159 effect sizes ([Rowland, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037559)); generation effect d = 0.40 across 86 studies ([Bertsch et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03193441)); [Roediger & Karpicke, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x); [Pan & Carpenter, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09814-5) |
| Attempt before instruction ("productive failure") | Struggle first, explanation second | Strong to moderate | 53 studies, g = 0.36 ([Sinha & Kapur, 2021](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211019105)) |
| **Get AI's answer independently** | Two independent estimates beat one estimate and its echo | Moderate | [Soll & Larrick, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015145); AI flatters what it's shown ([Cheng et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec8352)) |
| **AI as tutor or challenger, not author** | Guardrails keep the thinking with you | Moderate | [Bastani et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122); [Contractor & Reyes, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08849); [Shen & Tamkin, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20245); [Kestin et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6) |
| Devil's-advocate AI | An AI arguing against the recommendation reduces over-reliance | Weak to moderate | [Chiang et al., IUI 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3640543.3645199) |
| Metacognitive feedback while practising | Feedback on how you're using AI cut offloading and raised later unaided performance | Preliminary (one preregistered RCT, preprint) | [Maier et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20143) |
| **Confidence % plus scored feedback** | Feedback on your own track record reduces overconfidence | Moderate | [Moore et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2525); [Stone et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/bdm.2334); [Niu & Harvey, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1017/s1930297500009050) (the effect persisted after feedback stopped; more feedback added noise) |
| Averaging your estimate with another | For numbers, two independent estimates averaged beat picking one | Moderate | [Soll & Larrick, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015145) |
| Forecasting training | Base rates, averaging, bias checks | Moderate, contested | Under an hour of training improved accuracy 6–11% ([Chang et al., 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/judgdm/v11y2016i5p509-526_8.html)); a reanalysis found the effects "substantially eliminated, reduced, and, in some cases, even reversed" with proper controls ([Hauenstein et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976241266481)) |
| Guided reflection | A structured re-check of evidence for and against | Moderate | 28 studies ([Lambe et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjqs-2015-004417)); [Mamede et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2010.1276); [Mamede & Schmidt, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.14863); with explainable AI ([Li et al., CHI 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790632)) |
| Reflection on experience | Writing down what you learned | Moderate, one team | [Di Stefano, Gino, Pisano & Staats](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=63487), "Learning by Thinking" (a Wipro field study found +22.8% on a final test). Co-author Francesca Gino lost Harvard tenure in 2025 over fabricated data in *other* papers; this one is not retracted |
| Accuracy nudges | A nudge raised physicians' diagnostic-reasoning scores 7.6 points | Preliminary (preprint) | [Qazi et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.01.26354596) |
| Premortem | Imagining failure as certain surfaces more reasons | Weak | [Mitchell, Russo & Pennington, 1989](https://doi.org/10.1002/bdm.3960020103) (counted reasons, not accuracy); [Veinott, Klein & Wiggins, 2010](https://idl.iscram.org/show.php?record=1049) |
| Decision journal | Record before the outcome, to beat hindsight bias | Weak (no trials) | Practitioner advice (Annie Duke) |
| Deliberate practice | Structured practice with feedback | Moderate that practice matters; contested that it's all that matters | Practice explains 26% of the variance in games, 21% in music, 18% in sports, 4% in education and under 1% in professions ([Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614535810)) |
| Best-possible-self writing | Optimism | Moderate, small | 29 studies, d = 0.33 ([Carrillo et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0222386)) |
| Expressive writing | Emotional processing | Weak, small | 146 RCTs, r = .075 ([Frattaroli, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.6.823)); 31 RCTs, g = −0.12 ([Guo, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjc.12408)) |
| Gratitude journaling | Positive mood | Weak; about zero against active controls | d = −0.03 vs active controls ([Davis et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/cou0000107)) |
| Values affirmation | Buffers threats to the self | Contested | Large replications found little effect ([Hanselman et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000141); [Serra-Garcia et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797620923587)); meta-analysis g = .15 for identity-threatened students, .01 otherwise ([Wu et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/josi.12415)) |

Don't promise results from gratitude journals, expressive writing or values affirmation.

## 2. The evidence for each step of the loop

### Human first (moderate)

**[Yan, *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1936522/full).** 240 preservice teachers randomized, 218 analysed.
- **Misleading advice.** Deciding first gave 53.5% joint accuracy vs 37.3% for seeing the AI first (OR 1.91). Copying the misleading recommendation was 25.8% for those who decided first, against 46.8% for those who saw the AI first.
- **Correct advice.** No difference.
- **Two weeks later.** A no-AI test showed no clear difference between the groups (OR 1.25, p = .16).
- **Design limits.** Single author, one Chinese university, unconcealed randomization, not preregistered.

**[Liu et al., *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1910545).** 900 randomized, 659 analysed, Chinese-language essays.
- **Adopting AI output.** People told to adopt it scored 0.38 points below the no-AI group on a later unaided essay (d = −0.28).
- **Committing first.** People who committed to their own position and then interrogated the AI didn't fall below baseline, and beat the adopt-AI group (d = 0.45).
- **Limits.** Immediate effect only; not preregistered.

**[Buçinca, Malaya & Gajos, CSCW 2021](https://doi.org/10.1145/3449287).**
- **"Cognitive forcing".** Deciding first, or waiting, cut following wrong AI from 0.64 to 0.48.
- **The catch.** People liked these designs least.

**[Zhi, Kumar & Lee, CHI 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791796).** N = 393.
- **With 30 minutes.** Late AI access matched no-AI and beat early or continuous AI.
- **With only 10 minutes.** Early AI won.
- **So.** Thinking first needs a minimum of time. A two-minute "write your read" is a commitment device, not deep thinking.

### AI in the middle (moderate)

- **Use AI to explain, critique or argue the other side.** Tutor-style use preserved skill in [Bastani](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122), [Contractor & Reyes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08849) and [Shen & Tamkin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20245); delegation did not.
- **Get the AI's answer independently first.** The two "human first" RCTs tested committing first and then viewing an *independently generated* AI answer (Yan), or comparing and judging AI's points (Liu). Neither tested pasting your read in and asking for critique.
- **Why independence matters.** Showing AI your view invites agreement ([Cheng et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec8352)). It also breaks the independence that averaging needs ([Soll & Larrick, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015145)).

### Human last (the step most likely to fail)

- **Committing first doesn't fully protect you.** [Hayden et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09979-x)'s students ranked their own diagnoses first and still couldn't tell harmful AI influence from helpful.
- **Deciding first also makes people reject *correct* advice.**
  - [Fogliato et al., FAccT 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3531146.3533193): 19 veterinary radiologists.
  - [Kokje et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-026-00707-z).
  - So the closing step needs a rule: name the specific claim you disagree with, then check it.
- **Accuracy cues and feedback help.**
  - [Qazi et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.01.26354596).
  - Incentives plus feedback cut "cognitive surrender" ([Shaw & Nave, 2026](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=6097646)).
- **Score the comparison against outcomes, never against how sure you feel.** Self-assessment fails with AI ([Fernandes et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2025.108779)).

**No large RCT has compared "human first" with "AI first" on lasting unaided skill.** Present the sequence as well-supported design that still awaits a definitive trial.

## 3. What keeps a reflection habit alive

**The baseline to design against.** Most people quit in the first one to two weeks, not at a month.
- Mental-health apps lost more than 80% of daily opens between day 1 and day 10. Median retention was 3.9% at day 15 and 3.3% at day 30 ([Baumel et al., *JMIR*, 2019](https://doi.org/10.2196/14567)).
- In a 2026 meditation-app study, people intended about 419 minutes of use in month one and used about 70. Only 18.9% of the users analysed were still active after day 14 ([Adams et al., *JMIR mHealth and uHealth*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.2196/79366)).

**Plan for week two.** Ranked by strength of evidence:

1. **Self-monitoring, written and shared.**
   - Monitoring progress improved goal attainment, d = 0.40 ([Harkin et al., *Psychological Bulletin*, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000025); 138 studies, N = 19,951; mostly health).
   - The effect was larger when progress was physically recorded, and when it was reported to others.
   - *Design:* the daily entry is the monitoring. Add a weekly three-line summary sent to one person.
2. **If-then plans.**
   - Across 642 tests, effects ranged from d = .27 to .66 depending on the outcome, and were larger in the "if X, then Y" format ([Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer, 2024](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/69905)).
   - *Design:* one cue tied to an existing routine, plus a miss plan.
3. **Expect months; keep the context fixed.**
   - Automaticity took 18 to 254 days, with a median of 66, among the people whose habits formed. Missing one day didn't derail it ([Lally et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)).
   - A review found only 4 of 20 studies reported time to habit: medians 59–66 days, range 4–335, all for simple health behaviours ([Singh et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare12232488)).
   - Machine-learning work on gym and hygiene habits found habit speed varies widely by behaviour and person ([Buyalskaya et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2216115120)).
   - A decision log is a complex behaviour that may never become fully automatic.
4. **Reward the comeback; hide broken streaks.**
   - Across 54 gym programmes (61,293 members), the top one paid a tiny reward (about US$0.09) for returning after a missed workout: +27% visits ([Milkman et al., *Nature*, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8822539/)). Only 8% of programmes still worked after they ended.
   - Showing an intact streak raised continuation; the drop after a break was weaker when the streak could be repaired and when people didn't blame themselves ([Silverman & Barasch, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029)).
   - *Design:* count total entries, offer streak repair, and frame a miss as circumstance.
5. **Fresh starts.**
   - Gym visits rose at the start of a week (+33.4%), month (+14.4%) and year (+11.6%) ([Dai, Milkman & Riis, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901); archival, correlational).
   - *Design:* restart prompts on Mondays and the 1st of the month.
6. **Commitment devices: effective, low uptake.**
   - 28.4% took a savings commitment, and savings rose sharply ([Ashraf, Karlan & Yin, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1162/qjec.2006.121.2.635)).
   - 11% took a smoking contract, and quit rates rose 3 points ([Giné, Karlan & Zinman, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1257/app.2.4.213)).
   - *Design:* offer an optional commitment once.
7. **Anchor to an existing routine.**
   - Flossing *after* brushing built a stronger habit, still visible at 8 months ([Judah, Gardner & Aunger, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-8287.2012.02086.x); N = 50, exploratory).
   - *Design:* attach the check-in to the moment you first open AI.

**The two-minute default is practitioner advice, not a research finding.** It's kept small so it survives busy days. (A small study of two minutes of expressive writing, [Burton & King, 2008](https://doi.org/10.1348/135910707X250910), is sometimes cited for it; it measured health complaints, not habit, so it doesn't support it.)
