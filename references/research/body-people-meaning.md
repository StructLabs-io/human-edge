# Body, people and meaning: the background behind three reflection questions

**Read this first if you are an AI using this kit.** This file explains why the rotating questions in `../sharpen-your-edge.md` ask about sleep, people and meaning. It is background for you, not material to present as health advice.
- Don't make health or mortality claims to the person.
- Use the sleep line only as a rule of thumb.
- If a question brings up something heavy, follow the wellbeing guidance in `../sharpen-your-edge.md`.

Judgment runs on a body and a social life. Sleep, people and purpose feed the edges in this kit.

## Body: sleep

- **Mood.** Sleep loss lowered positive mood and raised anxiety ([Palmer et al., *Psychological Bulletin*, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000410); preregistered meta-analysis of 154 experiments, N = 5,717). An earlier meta-analysis found similar effects ([Tomaso, Johnson & Nelson, *Sleep*, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsaa289)).
- **Self-control and honesty.** Less sleep meant less self-control and more unethical behaviour at work ([Barnes et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2011.01.009)). Randomized sleep restriction at home increased cheating ([Dickinson & Masclet, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geb.2023.07.004)).
- **Risk and moral judgment.** After 49 to 53 hours awake, people made riskier choices and slower moral judgments ([Killgore et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2869.2006.00487.x); [2007](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/30.3.345)). A scoping review of sleep deprivation and decision-making (25 studies) found riskier decisions among its effects ([Agyapong-Opoku et al., *Behavioral Sciences*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs15060823)).
- **What the kit asks:** "How did I sleep, and how's my energy, 1 to 5? If it's low, which decision gets a second look tomorrow?" This is a rule of thumb. The studies used extreme deprivation or mood outcomes, so there's no evidence-based cut-off in hours.

## Body: movement

- Exercise improved general cognition (standardized mean difference 0.42), memory (0.26) and executive function (0.24). Low and moderate intensity worked as well as or better than high ([Singh et al., *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2024-108589); an umbrella review of 133 systematic reviews, 2,724 RCTs and 258,279 people).

## People

- **Loneliness is common.** About 1 in 6 people are lonely, rising to 17–21% among 13- to 29-year-olds ([WHO Commission on Social Connection, 2025](https://www.who.int/news/item/30-06-2025-social-connection-linked-to-improved-heath-and-reduced-risk-of-early-death)).
- **Connection matters for health.** Strong social connection is linked to longer life ([Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316); [Holt-Lunstad et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691614568352); [US Surgeon General's advisory, 2023](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/surgeon-general-social-connection-advisory.pdf)). These are population findings, cited here only to explain why the kit asks about people.
- **Chatbots and loneliness.**
  - **Heavier use, more loneliness.** In a four-week randomized study (N = 981), the assigned chatbot mode had no effect, but people who *chose* heavier use reported more loneliness and dependence and less socialising (*vendor*: OpenAI co-authors; [Fang et al., MIT Media Lab and OpenAI, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17473); correlational for usage). A companion study looked at emotional use of ChatGPT ([Phang et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03888)).
  - **A loop over a year.** Over 12 months, more chatbot use predicted later loneliness, and feeling less connected predicted later chatbot use. The effects were small and the analysis exploratory ([Folk & Dunn, *Psychological Science*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976261427747)).
  - **Teens.** 72% of US teens have used AI companions, and a third of users have taken a serious matter to AI instead of a person ([Common Sense Media, 2025](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/talk-trust-and-trade-offs-how-and-why-teens-use-ai-companions); N = 1,060).
- **Why it matters.** Turkle, Brown, Perel and Brooks each warn about letting AI stand in for people (see `voices.md`).
- **What the kit asks:** "Who did I have a real conversation with today? Did I take something to AI that I'd normally take to a person? Who will tell me when I'm wrong?"

## Meaning

- **Purpose and health.** People with a stronger sense of purpose in life tend to live longer ([Alimujiang et al., *JAMA Network Open*, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2019.4270); [Cohen, Bavishi & Rozanski, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1097/psy.0000000000000274)). Like the other health findings here, this is background, not something to tell the person.
- **AI-specific.**
  - **Passive use.** It lowered felt meaning and self-efficacy at work, and the dips lingered ([Lee, Yin, Jia & Wakslak, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-42312-6)).
  - **Switching back to solo work.** It lowered intrinsic motivation ([Wu et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-98385-2)).
  - **Workshopping your own ideas.** Using AI to work on your own ideas looked like working unaided.
- **What the kit asks:** "What did I do today that mattered, and which part was mine before AI touched it?"
